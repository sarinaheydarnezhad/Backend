انتزاع و حد شاهد

در انتزاع، رابطهٔ مشترک مثال‌ها را نگه می‌داریم و جزئیات بی‌ربط را حذف می‌کنیم؛ ولی نتیجه نباید دامنه یا قطعیت بیشتری از متن پیدا کند. برای هر گزینه بپرسید چه شاهدی آن را پشتیبانی می‌کند. پاسخ باز به معنی بی‌معیار بودن پاسخ نیست؛ می‌توان چند تفسیر پذیرفت و در عین حال تفسیر ناسازگار با متن را رد کرد. در رابطهٔ بندها، توضیح مثال، قید محدودکننده و نتیجه را جدا کنید. شمار یا طول گفت‌وگو به‌تنهایی کیفیت یادگیری را اندازه نمی‌گیرد.

نمونهٔ حل‌شده

پس از خواندن، یک جملهٔ مشاهده، یک جملهٔ نسبت‌داده‌شده به گوینده و یک نتیجهٔ پشتیبانی‌نشده را از هم جدا کنید. سپس بند پشتیبان هر پاسخ را مشخص کنید و قیدهای آن را در بازنویسی نگه دارید.

منبع خبر و شرط لازم

در متن خبری، گویندهٔ ادعا، شاهد و وضعیت تصمیم را جدا یادداشت کنید. گفتهٔ منبع، برآورد، مشاهدهٔ اندازه‌گیری‌شده و تصمیم نهایی هم‌ارز نیستند. نبود شاهد برای یک ادعا، اثبات نقیض آن نیست. عبارتی مانند only when شرط لازم را معرفی می‌کند و از آن نباید کافی بودن شرط را نتیجه گرفت. واژهٔ promising امید محتاطانه را می‌رساند، نه تضمین آینده. در تعمیم از یک دورهٔ مشاهده، دامنهٔ زمانی و عوامل تغییرپذیر را حفظ کنید.

Reading passage

At a fictional museum, the education team replaced a long introduction to an exhibition with three short questions printed on a card. Visitors could choose one question, look for an object that helped them answer it, and compare their choice with a companion. The card did not identify a single correct object. Its purpose was to encourage visitors to explain the connection they had made.

In a small observation exercise, the team recorded more conversations near the entrance after the cards appeared. That finding did not show that visitors learned more facts. Conversation length had been recorded, but no test of factual knowledge had been given. Nor could the team exclude the possibility that a new seating area, installed during the same week, helped people stay and talk.

Some staff worried that a question without a single answer would make the exhibition seem vague. Others argued that an answer could be open without being arbitrary: visitors still needed evidence from an object to support it. A claim about a painted scene, for example, could be challenged if the described detail was absent. On this view, several interpretations might be reasonable while some remained unsupported.

The team decided to keep the cards and to study how they were used. Its report distinguished two goals: helping people begin a conversation and helping them improve an explanation. The first observations offered limited evidence for the first goal. Evaluating the second would require attention to what visitors said, rather than only how long they spoke.

What was the main purpose of the museum question card?

  1. To prove that seating never affects behaviour
  2. To identify the only correct object in each room
  3. To prevent discussion between companions
  4. To encourage visitors to support a connection between a question and an object
  5. To replace every explanation with a factual test
Answer and explanation

D. The first paragraph states that visitors should explain a connection; the card does not prescribe a single correct object.

Why could the team not confidently attribute the longer conversations only to the cards?

  1. Every visitor took a factual test
  2. A seating area was introduced during the same week
  3. No conversations occurred
  4. The report proved the cards had no effect
  5. All visitors had rejected the cards
Answer and explanation

B. The seating change is an alternative contributor. The text does not establish that the cards had no effect.

Which general principle best matches the staff argument in paragraph three?

  1. Evidence matters only when one answer exists
  2. Descriptions cannot ever be challenged
  3. An interpretation can be open to alternatives while still constrained by evidence
  4. Open questions have no educational purpose
  5. Every interpretation is equally well supported
Answer and explanation

C. Staff distinguish open answers from arbitrary ones: several may be reasonable, but object details still constrain them.

What distinction does the last paragraph emphasise?

  1. The two goals require exactly the same evidence
  2. Visitors cannot discuss objects while seated
  3. Observation is identical to a factual test
  4. Longer speech necessarily means more factual learning
  5. Starting a conversation differs from improving the quality of an explanation
Answer and explanation

E. The report separates initiation from explanation quality, and says the second needs attention to content rather than duration alone.

In the final sentence, what does "the second" refer to?

  1. A second correct object
  2. The second week of installation
  3. The goal of improving an explanation
  4. A second seating area
  5. The second visitor entering the museum
Answer and explanation

C. The two goals were just enumerated. The second is helping people improve an explanation.

Reading passage

A fictional local newspaper reports that a school has finished its first season of collecting rainwater from a roof for use in its garden. The caretaker estimated that the system supplied about one third of the garden water during that season. The figure was based on tank readings, not on a prediction of rainfall for future years.

The school said it had reduced the amount of treated mains water used outdoors. It did not claim that the collected water was suitable for drinking, and the newspaper found no published test establishing that it was. Garden use and drinking-water use involve different requirements; success at one does not by itself establish suitability for the other.

An environmental club proposed installing a second tank. The caretaker supported examining the idea but pointed out that storage was not the only constraint. During long dry periods, even a larger tank could eventually be empty. During heavy rain, the amount reaching a tank also depended on the area and condition of the roof and gutters. Extra capacity would help only when there was additional water available to store.

The governing board has requested records from another season before deciding. One board member called the first results promising, while adding that a second season would not remove all uncertainty about later weather. The current report therefore describes a useful local trial and a proposal still under review, rather than a decision already made.

What is the status of the claim that the system supplied about one third of garden water?

  1. It is a caretaker estimate based on readings from one season
  2. It is a statement that all garden water came from rain
  3. It is an independently established drinking-water standard
  4. It is a decision to install a second tank
  5. It is a guaranteed minimum for every future year
Answer and explanation

A. The first paragraph attributes an estimate to the caretaker and specifies its season-specific basis.

Under the caretaker's reasoning, when could extra storage capacity help?

  1. Whenever weather records are missing
  2. Only after all mains water has been banned
  3. When additional collectable water is available to store
  4. Even if no water can reach the tank
  5. Only when the roof area becomes zero
Answer and explanation

C. The third paragraph explicitly makes extra capacity useful only if extra water is available.

Which conclusion about drinking water is justified by the report?

  1. Every sample has been proved poisonous
  2. The garden trial alone does not establish suitability for drinking
  3. The newspaper tested all the water itself
  4. The school claimed drinking-water certification
  5. The collected water has been proved safe to drink
Answer and explanation

B. The report says there is no published test establishing suitability. This is not proof either of safety or of contamination.

What has the governing board decided?

  1. To request records from another season before deciding about expansion
  2. To install the tank immediately
  3. To guarantee next year's rainfall
  4. To end all garden watering permanently
  5. To approve drinking use
Answer and explanation

A. The final paragraph reports a request for more records, not a final expansion decision.

How does the reference to future weather qualify the promising results?

  1. It guarantees the next season will be identical
  2. It limits how confidently one can generalise from the observed season
  3. It proves that tank readings were fabricated
  4. It establishes that more records have no value
  5. It contradicts the existence of the garden
Answer and explanation

B. Positive observed results do not eliminate future variability. Additional observations can help without removing all uncertainty.
